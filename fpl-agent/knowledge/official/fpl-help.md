Fantasy Premier League (FPL) is a game where you pick real Premier League players and score points based on how they perform in their actual matches.
You start by building a squad, choosing a starting line-up, selecting a captain for that week and then managing your team across the entire season. You can play against millions of managers worldwide and create leagues to compete with friends, family and colleagues.
There's lots of information to take in when you start playing. Begin with the basics below, then use the Rules, FAQ and Scout for more detail.
On web, you can also select the help icon whenever it appears to view information about the current page.

Fantasy Premier League in 60 seconds

In FPL, you are given a budget to build a squad of Premier League players. Your players score points for real-life actions such as goals, assists and clean sheets.
The Premier League fixtures are grouped into 38 Gameweeks over the season. You choose 11 players from your squad of 15 to score points for you in the Gameweek, place the rest on your bench and select a captain, whose score is doubled.
Transfers let you improve your squad, react to injuries and target better fixtures.
You can play one of four chips to boost your Gameweek score over the course of the season.
The aim is to score as many points as possible across the season and climb the rankings, while competing in leagues against other managers.

Step 1: Build your squad

The first step is to select a squad of 15 players while staying within the budget of £100.0m.
A good initial squad is usually built from:
15 players who regularly start matches for their teams.
Attacking players, such as forwards and midfielders, who are likely to score goals or provide assists.
Defenders and goalkeepers who are likely to get clean sheets, earned when their team prevents their opponent from scoring a goal.
A strong starting 11, with some good options on the bench in case starting players do not play.
One or two proven players with a strong record for consistent Fantasy points who can be captained each Gameweek for double points.

Step 2: Pick your starting line-up

Each Gameweek, you choose your starting 11 from your squad of 15.
That starting line-up is the group of players who can score points for you in the Gameweek. Your substitutes are placed in a chosen order on the bench and will come into play, in that order, if one of your starting 11 does not play.
When picking your team, consider:
Who is most likely to start for their team?
Which goalkeeper and defenders have a fixture that gives them the best chance of a clean sheet?
Which attacking players have a fixture that gives them the best chance of a goal or an assist?
Is your bench in the right order?
See FPL Basics Explained: Managing your team

Step 3: Choose your captain

Your captain is one of the most important decisions in FPL because their Gameweek points are doubled.
You should usually captain a player who:
Is very likely to start
Has a strong potential for one or more returns of points (a goal, assist or a clean sheet)
You must also select a vice-captain. Use the same selection criteria, as your vice-captain's points will be doubled if your captain does not play in the Gameweek.

Step 4: Understand how points are scored

Players score points based on what they do in Premier League matches.
Common ways to score points include:
Playing minutes
Scoring goals
Earning assists
Earning clean sheets
Making saves
Earning defensive contribution points
Earning bonus points
Defensive contribution points are designed to give players less likely to score goals or assists another way of earning points for your team. They are awarded for completing defensive actions in a match, including tackles, clearances and blocked shots. Midfielders can also earn defensive contribution points for regaining possession for their team through recoveries.
Bonus points are awarded at the end of the match to the three top performers. This is based on the Bonus Points System (BPS), which uses actions from the match. Players who score goals or provide assists are normally best placed to earn bonus points, but defenders and goalkeepers can also earn them if they keep a clean sheet.
See FPL Basics Explained: Scoring points

Step 5: Make transfers to improve your squad

Transfers let you change your squad during the season.
You can use transfers to:
Replace injured or suspended players
Bring in players with better fixtures
React to changes in form
Move money between positions
Target new players who have become regular starters
You get one free transfer per Gameweek but can make further transfers if you wish. Additional transfers in a Gameweek deduct 4 points from your Gameweek score; this is often called a "points hit".
If you choose not to use a free transfer in a Gameweek, it will roll over to the following Gameweek. You can keep rolling transfers up to a maximum total of five free transfers.
See FPL Basics Explained: How to make transfers

Step 6: Use chips at the right time

Chips are special boosters that can help your team at key moments in the season. You can only play one chip in a Gameweek.
The four chips are:
Wildcard: Make unlimited transfers to rebuild your entire squad.
Free Hit: Change your squad for one Gameweek only, then return to your previous squad.
Bench Boost: Score points from all 15 players in your squad for one Gameweek.
Triple Captain: Your captain scores triple points instead of double.
There are two sets of chips: one for the first half of the season and one for the second. Unused chips do not carry over, so any chip not used within its available period is lost.
See FPL Basics Explained: How to use your chips

Step 7: Join leagues and compete with friends

Leagues are where FPL becomes more social and competitive.
You are automatically entered into some leagues based on your nationality and your favourite Premier League club. However, you can join and create more leagues to compete against friends, family and colleagues. Each league also has a knockout cup competition.
To get started, click on Leagues and Cups and then choose either to Create a League or, if you have been invited to a league, click “Join a League” then “Join an Invitational League & Cup” and then enter the code when prompted.
See FPL Basics Explained: Leagues and cups

Your Gameweek checklist

FPL is played one Gameweek at a time, and each Gameweek has a deadline which is an hour and a half before the kick-off of the first fixture.
Be sure to check the Status Page for the date and time of the next Gameweek deadline and those that follow.
Use this checklist before each Gameweek deadline to make sure your team is ready.
Have you picked your starting XI?
Have you selected your captain and vice-captain?
Are your substitutes in the right order?
Are any of your players injured, suspended or unlikely to start?
Do you need to make a transfer?
Are you considering using a chip?

FPL glossary

FPL has a lot of terms that you will learn as you play. Here are some that you are most likely to see when playing the game, reading articles or talking to other managers.
Gameweek: A round of Premier League fixtures in which your players score points.
Deadline: The time by which you must make team changes for the next Gameweek.
Squad: Your full group of players.
Starting 11: The 11 players selected in your starting line-up.
Bench: Your substitutes.
Captain: The player whose points are doubled.
Vice-captain: The player who becomes captain if your captain does not play.
Transfer: Replacing one player in your squad with another.
Free transfer: A transfer that does not cost points.
Points hit: A points deduction from your Gameweek total for making an extra transfer.
Chip: A special boost that can be used during the season.
Wildcard: A chip that allows unlimited transfers.
Free Hit: A chip that changes your squad for one Gameweek only.
Bench Boost: A chip that lets your substitutes score points.
Triple Captain: A chip that triples your captain's score.
Clean sheet: When a team does not concede a goal to their opponent.
Defensive contributions: Defensive actions that can be rewarded if a player reaches a certain total in a match.
Return: When a player earns a goal, an assist or a clean sheet.
Bonus points: Extra points awarded to the best-performing players in a match.
Form: A measure of recent Fantasy performance.
Fixture: A scheduled Premier League match.
Double Gameweek: A Gameweek where some teams play more than once.
Blank Gameweek: A Gameweek where some teams do not play.
Price change: When a player's FPL price rises or falls.
